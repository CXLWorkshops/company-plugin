# Content Recycling Best Practices

Operational distillation of the 15-lesson CXL Content Recycling course. Companion to [[content repurposing frameworks]]: that note covers repurposing methods per format; this one carries the course's system for planning, producing, and recycling content as one workflow. The course predates the current AI landscape (~2020 era), so read it in two layers: the planning/system principles (§1–§4, §6) transfer intact; the channel tactics (§3, §5) need the 2026 corrections in §7. Named tools from the course era (CoSchedule, Adobe Spark, Mobi, etc.) are category examples only — verify with a web search before using or citing any specific tool.

## 1. The mental model

- **One topic, many channels.** Content recycling means producing a piece once and pushing it out across channels: blog → podcast, webinar, social snippets, infographics; multiple posts → ebook, course, report.
- **Four payoffs:** efficiency (more output from the same resources), reach (audiences who don't read blogs but do listen to podcasts or scroll social), channel amplification (social drives blog, blog drives newsletter, newsletter resurfaces posts), and consistency (recycled content fills the calendar, and audiences build around predictable output).
- **Recycling is also a testing mechanism.** A blog post or social update that catches on is evidence a bigger investment (course, book, season) has legs; a flop is a cheap lesson.
- **Formats cluster.** Text formats (social updates ↔ blog posts ↔ ebooks) transpose easily between each other; video → audio → social snippets is another natural cluster. Pick formats by audience fit, reusability, and engagement. (The course's warning that crossing clusters is "much more work" is largely obsolete — see §7.)

## 2. The modular content system

The structural insight the whole system rests on. Content has three levels:

1. **Snippets of value** (micro): stats, quotes, tips, pull-out statements. What gets shared on social. People share what makes them look smart or funny, or what validates a belief they already hold; write snippets accordingly.
2. **Single subjects**: one blog post / video / podcast episode — really a collection of snippets assembled around one question.
3. **Topics**: 6–12 subjects on one theme, combinable into an ebook, course, or podcast season (roughly four hours of content).

Plan top-down (topic → subjects → snippets), write bottom-up. Content that recycles well is modular by construction: lists, stats, quotable statements.

**Idea supply:** keep a running ideas list at all three levels. Feed it from subject experts, customer-service staff (real questions customers ask), customer surveys, social listening, and above all keyword research: autocompletes and question queries show what people actually search. Not the subject expert? Interview like a journalist, research and cite stats (quoting a stat requires no expertise), ask the audience for input, or commission guest posts with assigned subjects.

## 3. The core workflow

- **The foundation triangle: blog → newsletter ← social.** The course puts the blog at the heart (search traffic, demonstrated expertise, easiest signup surface); the newsletter is the primary CTA and long-term retention channel; social feeds the top. Each channel recycles the others' material. (2026 correction: the *owned-hub-plus-satellites* shape survives, but the hub doesn't have to be a blog and social's job has changed — see §7.)
- **The escalation ladder:** add audio versions of posts (cheap, low competition), then downloads/checklists/infographics built from post content, then full **recycling projects**: plan a 6–12 post series on one topic → release posts + extracted social updates + audio → consolidate into ebook/audiobook (newsletter incentive) → podcast season → course. One planning decision produces months of output and a content funnel.
- **Every channel moves people one step down the funnel.** The hub's primary job is email signups; satellites' primary job is feeding the hub.

## 4. Test before you invest

- **Don't trust surveys**: people say yes to anything offered. Demand a tangible action instead.
- **The landing-page test**: before building a course/book/season, publish a landing page for the not-yet-existing product with a "notify me" email CTA. Email addresses are commitment; page hits are not. Also forces you to articulate the audience and promise.
- **Cheaper probes**: publish 1–2 posts from the planned topic and watch engagement; float the idea on social; monitor which past posts overperformed and let them nominate the next topic; check site search and keyword volume.
- **Test proportionally**: big projects always get tested; individual posts only when in doubt. A webinar is itself a cheap test of a course idea.

## 5. Production playbooks

- **Write in two separated modes** ("write drunk, edit sober"): outline from the ideas list → uninterrupted draft (distraction-free editor, markdown) → grammar/readability pass → text-to-speech readback to catch literal errors → format and publish. Never edit while drafting; keep a 2–3 week backlog; replace approval chains with pre-agreed guidelines from legal/compliance. Target: idea to published in a couple of hours.
- **Format posts for scanning and extraction.** People read 20–28% of a page (Nielsen Norman Group): compelling title + summary up top, headings as "little adverts", descriptive link text, a table of contents, bullets, bolded key phrases, shareable pull-out quotes, imagery. Keep readers moving with related links, body links, and CTAs embedded *in* the content, not sidebars. This doubles as snippet preparation — a well-formatted post has its social updates pre-extracted — and overlaps heavily with [[geo optimization best practices]] §2: the same formatting that helps humans scan helps LLMs extract. Apply both passes together.
- **Social: extract, don't syndicate.** Re-posting whole articles to LinkedIn/Medium builds platform-owned loyalty and strips your CTAs; extracting quotes, stats, images, questions, and polls performs better. (How the extract links back has changed — see §7 on native-first.)
- **Graphics from posts, three ways:** reuse example screenshots prepared during writing, turn quotes into typographic images, turn lists/stats into infographics.
- **Audio: start with the straight read.** A read-aloud version of each post is the cheapest new channel; conversational podcasts are more engaging but much more work. Decent mic, quiet room, talk to one person with energy; let a podcast host handle distribution and give you an embeddable player for the post. (The course's "avoid robotic TTS" rule has flipped — see §7.)
- **Video: length follows format, not source.** Animation under ~90s for concepts; talking heads under ~10 min (a blog post read aloud, conveniently); screencasts under ~20 min, face in corner, don't read slides; informal phone video under ~90s for testimonials. Eye contact (teleprompter), decent camera, lapel mic, and lighting are the quality levers.
- **Long-form combos: webinar first, then course.** A webinar needs ~4 posts of material, tolerates low production values, is interactive, and doubles as a course test. Courses take more but confer more authority (a price tag raises perceived value even if you discount to free); plan the outline before recording, keep lessons ~10 min (~1,300 words at ~130 wpm), add supporting materials, distribute beyond your own site.
- **Ebooks/books: plan chapters even if you only blog today.** A chapter ≈ 6 posts; a book ≈ 50,000 words. Write gap-filling chapters as blog posts first ("never write something once"), draft in markdown for painless PDF/EPUB export, use the ebook as the newsletter incentive; physical books are memorable and nearly impossible to throw away.

## 6. Email closes the loop

- Emails deliver value first, sales second; value is why people stay subscribed until they're ready to buy.
- **Recycle by theme, not by post**: pull threads from several old posts into one email that links back to them, resurfacing archive content.
- Segment where a slice of the audience shares a worldview; build an **onboarding series** that walks new subscribers through your best existing material.
- Reverse flow: an email that gets unusual response becomes a blog post or something bigger. Post newsletters online (unlisted) so they're shareable and carry their own signup CTA.
- Overlay CTAs on exit intent or scroll depth, never on page load.

## 7. What's changed by 2026 — vault corrections and additions

The course's system layer holds; these channel-tactic claims don't. Apply these on top:

- **Social is native-first now; "drive blog traffic" is dead as social's primary job.** Platforms suppress outbound links, and audiences consume in-feed. The extraction principle survives fully — snippets of value are exactly what native posts are made of — but the mechanic is *extract and re-package natively* (carousel, native long-post, short video), with the link demoted to a comment, profile, or occasional CTA. Social's realistic jobs in 2026: audience building, authority, and newsletter signups via profile/funnel, not click-throughs per post.
- **The hub doesn't have to be a blog.** AI search and zero-click results have cut blog click-through; the durable hub is the channel you own end-to-end. In this vault, [[b2b ai tactics newsletter]] is the hub and everything derives from it — which is the course's architecture with a different center. The blog's remaining distinct job is search/LLM retrievability, which is [[geo optimization best practices]] territory.
- **AI collapsed the effort economics.** Crossing format clusters (posts → video course, newsletter → carousel deck) is no longer "much more work"; drafting, transcription, voice, and design are near-free. Consequences: (a) pick formats by audience fit, not production cost; (b) planning and taste are the scarce inputs, which makes the §2 modular system *more* valuable, not less; (c) volume is no longer a moat — distinctive POV and proof-of-work (real data, real workflows) are.
- **Automated evergreen re-queues now get punished.** Recycling the same update on a fixed schedule reads as repetitive content to platform algorithms and followers alike. Recycle the *idea* on a schedule, but re-angle the packaging each time (new hook, new format, updated numbers).
- **The TTS rule flipped.** AI voices are good enough that the read-aloud audio channel and video voiceovers no longer require recording sessions. The remaining human premium is in conversational/personality formats, not straight reads.
- **Duplicate-content fear is overstated.** Syndication with canonical tags is handled fine by search engines; the real argument against wholesale syndication is the one that still stands — platform-owned loyalty and stripped CTAs.
- **Recycle for machines, not just channels.** Every recycled asset is also a retrieval surface for answer engines. When consolidating a topic arc into an ebook/landing page/webinar page, apply the [[geo optimization best practices]] passage rules so the consolidated asset can get cited, not just downloaded.
- **Short-form video is a first-class cluster.** The course's video section predates Shorts/Reels/TikTok as default distribution. A webinar or long video should yield 3–5 vertical clips as a standard step of the escalation ladder, not an optional extra.

## 8. Where to begin (and how this vault applies it)

Course's starting sequence: start small (hub + mailing list + only the most relevant social channels, maybe audio), plan one overarching topic before writing anything, design the mailing-list onboarding up front, and stay consistent for years, not weeks.

Mapping to this vault's system:

- [[b2b ai tactics newsletter]] plays the hub role (§7): the highest-signal asset from which everything derives. The snippet/subject/topic model argues for planning newsletter editions in **topic arcs** (6–12 editions on one theme) so they consolidate into an ebook, webinar series, or course with little extra work, feeding [[ai native marketer webinars]].
- The extract-don't-syndicate rule and the snippets-of-value checklist are the theory behind the `write-linkedin` skill's repurposing approach and [[content repurposing frameworks]] — applied native-first per §7.
- The landing-page test (§4) maps directly onto webinar topic selection for [[ai native marketer webinars]]: registrations are the commitment signal.
- The scannability/extraction rules in §5 pair with [[geo optimization best practices]] §2; run both passes together when revamping posts.
- Webinar recordings should yield vertical clips by default (§7) — ties to the standing commitment to cut 3–5 Shorts per webinar recording.
